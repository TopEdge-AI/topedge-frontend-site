# Page analysis prompt (owner's text, saved 18 Nov 2026)

Used every day on the day's page, after the on-page audit tool run. Input: that page's Search Console export (`gsc-export-*.csv` in the day's `page-audits/<page>/` folder). Output: `analysis-report.pdf` in the same folder.

---

Act as a senior SEO strategist, Google Search specialist, competitor analyst, and content optimization expert. I am providing you with a Google Search Console export for one specific webpage only. This export contains multiple search queries for that particular URL, including clicks, impressions, CTR, and average positions. Your task is to deeply analyze every keyword and identify why this page is receiving impressions but not generating enough organic clicks. Do not perform a website-wide audit or analyze unrelated pages.

Start by analyzing the complete GSC export, identifying high-impression keywords, zero-click queries, low-CTR keywords, and queries where the page has ranking potential but is not attracting clicks. Group related keywords according to their search intent and explain what users are actually looking for. Identify whether the problem is related to search intent, content relevance, title tags, meta descriptions, SERP competition, ranking positions, or missing information on the page.

Next, independently research Google SERPs for the most important keywords from the export. Identify the actual competing webpages ranking for those keywords and deeply analyze what they are doing differently. Examine their content structure, H1, H2 and H3 headings, important sections, information depth, examples, comparisons, tables, FAQs, original research, statistics, visuals, tools, trust signals, and other useful elements. Explain why these competing pages may be satisfying search intent better and why my page might be losing clicks or ranking opportunities. Provide actual competitor URLs and support your observations with reliable sources. Never invent competitor findings or assume that one particular feature is the reason for a ranking.

The most important part of your analysis is to recommend exactly what I should add or improve on my existing webpage. Identify missing sections, important subtopics, questions, entities, facts, comparisons, examples, tables, checklists, and other useful information that can make my page more comprehensive and relevant to its existing keywords. Provide exact H2 and H3 heading suggestions, explain what information each section should contain, which GSC keywords it targets, what user intent it satisfies, and how it can improve upon the content currently offered by competitors. Identify useful information that competitors are providing but my page is missing, as well as unique opportunities that can make my page more valuable than competing results. Do not recommend adding unnecessary content simply to increase word count or repeat keywords. Every recommendation must have a clear SEO and user-value justification.

Also provide three improved title tag options, two meta description options, a recommended H1, an improved introduction, and an optimized content structure for the existing page. Identify relevant internal linking opportunities, content improvements, technical SEO issues, and trust signals wherever they can be verified. Separate confirmed findings from assumptions and recommendations. Use the exact metrics available in my GSC export, and never fabricate data, rankings, statistics, competitor information, or research.

Finally, prioritize all recommendations according to their potential impact and implementation effort. Explain which changes I should implement first, which keywords each change targets, and how I should measure improvements through Google Search Console after implementation. Present everything in a clean, professional, detailed PDF report containing an executive summary, GSC keyword analysis, search intent analysis, competitor comparison, content gap analysis, exact recommended sections, on-page SEO improvements, and a prioritized action plan.

Important instructions: I will provide only one Google Search Console export for one webpage. You must identify the URL from the export, analyze all keywords associated with that URL, independently discover its Google SERP competitors, inspect the existing page where accessible, and conduct the complete research yourself. Do not ask me to manually provide competitor URLs, keyword lists, or page content. If the page URL is missing from the export, ask me only for that URL. If live research or page access is unavailable, clearly disclose the limitation instead of inventing findings. Your entire analysis must focus on improving this single webpage, increasing its relevance for its existing keywords, improving its organic click-through rate, and creating better opportunities to rank higher on Google.
